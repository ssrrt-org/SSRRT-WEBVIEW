"The cow is our mother. She nourishes us as we grow. Protecting her is not tradition — it is gratitude."

Tucked within the serene grounds of the Ashram, this Goshala — the Sanskrit word for a cow sanctuary — is home to more than 300 cows, bulls, and calves. It is a place where every animal is named, known, and loved. Where bulls that would otherwise have been sold to slaughterhouses live out their full natural lives in safety and dignity.

The Goshala is not merely a shelter. It is a living demonstration that the ancient wisdom of caring for cows is also the practical wisdom of building a sustainable future — with organic grass fields, planned biogas energy systems, and zero-waste composting cycles that enrich the very land on which the animals graze.

Amma's establishment and personal commitment to the Goshala is embodied leadership. She has built a sanctuary that honours the full life of every animal — a model of circular, zero-waste agriculture that the ancient rishis understood, and that modern sustainability advocates are urgently trying to recreate.

History of Goshala

From Two Cows to Three Hundred — Twenty Years of Growth

The Beginning: Two Cows and a Sacred Intention

Every great institution begins with a single step. Project Kaamadhenau began with two cows. Approximately twenty years ago, the Goshala was established at the Ashram with just two animals, a small shelter, a handful of devoted caregivers, and an enormous amount of love. But it was animated from the very first day by a clarity of vision and a depth of spiritual commitment that would allow it to grow far beyond what anyone might have initially imagined.

Those first two cows were not merely animals kept for their milk. They were the beginning of a covenant — a promise that here, at this Ashram, the ancient relationship between human beings and cows would be honoured in its fullness. That no animal would be discarded when their usefulness diminished. That bulls would not be sold to slaughter. That every cow, every calf, every bull would live out their natural lives in safety, dignity, and love.

Gaumata: The Universal Mother

In Sanskrit, the cow is called Gaumata — literally, "cow mother." The Rigveda calls her Aghnya, meaning "that which must not be killed." Lord Krishna himself, known as Govinda — the one who brings joy to cows — spent his childhood as a cowherd, tending cows with great love and devotion. When a Hindu household honours the cow, they participate in the same act of divine care that Krishna modelled thousands of years ago.

The very name of this project — Kaamadhenau — is derived from Kamadhenu, the mythical divine cow who has the power to grant any wish, fulfil any need, nourish any prayer. She is abundance itself. The generosity of the divine made manifest in the form of a gentle, giving creature.

Adopt a Cow

Srimad Sai RajRajeshwari Trust's Sacred Commitment to Gau Seva

Transform devotion into direct, tangible action — sponsor a cow's feed, shelter, and care, and receive updates on the life you help sustain.

Among the many streams of compassionate action flowing from SSRRT, few carry the depth of spiritual and cultural significance found in the "Adopt a Cow" initiative. Rooted in the timeless Indian tradition of Gau Seva — service to the cow — this program reflects Divine Mother Srimad Sai RajaRajeshwari's profound reverence for all living beings.

What the Adopt a Cow Initiative Involves

The program invites individuals, families, and organizations to take direct responsibility for the well-being of a cow — or contribute toward the collective care of cows housed at SSRRT's goshala — by sponsoring the costs associated with their feed, medical care, shelter, and daily upkeep.

Devotees can, in a very real sense, "adopt" a specific cow, receiving updates on her well-being, visiting her at the shelter, and knowing that their contribution directly sustains the life of a living being who might otherwise have been abandoned to suffering. This model transforms what might otherwise be viewed as routine charitable giving into a relationship of care and connection.

The Daily Reality of Care

A cow requires a steady, substantial supply of fodder and clean water every single day, along with mineral supplements to maintain her health. She requires regular veterinary check-ups, vaccinations, and treatment for illness and injury. She requires clean, spacious shelter that protects her from harsh sun in summer and biting cold in winter. For elderly or infirm cows — many of whom arrive in poor health after years of neglect — the needs are even greater.

"To care for a cow is to honour the mother in all her forms — the earth that feeds us, the waters that sustain us, the divine source from which all life flows."

Spiritual Dimensions of Gau Seva

In the Indian spiritual tradition, service to the cow is considered one of the most meritorious forms of seva. Divine Mother Srimad Sai RajaRajeshwari has often emphasized that true devotion must extend outward into the world through acts of care for all living beings. For many devotees, adopting a cow becomes a deeply personal spiritual practice — in memory of a loved one, in gratitude for a significant life event, or simply out of heartfelt love for animals.

The Many Faces of Goshala Volunteering

One of the most appealing aspects of the Volunteer for a Goshala program is the sheer diversity of ways in which people can contribute, allowing individuals of different ages, physical abilities, skills, and available time to find a meaningful role.

Daily Feeding & Fodder Preparation

Cutting and chopping green fodder, measuring and distributing concentrated feed, maintaining water troughs — the foundational rhythm of daily goshala care.

Shelter Cleaning & Maintenance

Regular cleaning of enclosures, maintaining clean water sources, and helping manage waste through composting programs that convert cow dung into valuable organic fertilizer.

Bathing & Grooming

Regular bathing and grooming help maintain skin and coat health, prevent parasitic infections, and offer an opportunity to check for any wounds or signs of illness.

Calf Care

Newborn and young calves require bottle-feeding, monitoring for illness, and the nurturing presence that young animals need to thrive — one of the most emotionally rewarding roles.

Administrative & Outreach Support

Record-keeping, coordinating schedules, managing donations, photographing cows for adoption programs, and spreading awareness through social media.

"There is something profoundly grounding about physical labor performed in service of another being who cannot repay you or thank you in words — and yet responds to it with the simple, wordless generosity of their nature."

Mother for Needy — Medical Care

Multi-Village Free Medical Center: A Lifeline for the Underserved

Access to basic healthcare remains one of the most persistent challenges in rural India. Recognizing this urgent gap, SSRRT established a Free Medical Center that today serves as a vital lifeline for four to five surrounding villages.

This center offers preliminary medical care completely free of cost — consultations, basic diagnostics, medicines for common ailments, and guidance on when more specialized care is needed. For elderly residents who can no longer travel long distances, for daily-wage laborers who cannot afford to lose a day's income, and for mothers and children who need regular check-ups, this facility has become indispensable.

In many of these villages, the nearest government hospital may be twenty, thirty, or even fifty kilometers away. SSRRT's medical center changes this equation entirely — removing the two greatest barriers to healthcare in rural India: distance and cost.

Beyond direct medical benefit, the center has become a trusted institution within the community, a place where people know they will be treated with dignity regardless of their ability to pay. A breadwinner who receives timely treatment for a treatable illness can continue to work and provide for their household; a child treated for a preventable disease can continue attending school.

Food for the Needy

Nourishing Body and Soul — One Warm Meal at a Time

Beyond the Trust's well-known Narayana Seva programme, Divine Mother Srimad Sai RajaRajeshwari has personally embodied the spirit of selfless giving by cooking and distributing food for hundreds of people who might otherwise go hungry — homeless individuals, vegetable vendors, small shopkeepers, and families facing financial hardship.

This is not charity delivered from a distance; it is an intimate, hands-on act of love, where food prepared with devotion becomes a vehicle for connection and dignity. Rather than distributing dry rations, food is prepared fresh with the same care one might give to feeding one's own family — because every person deserves the dignity of a warm, freshly prepared meal.

"True wealth is measured not by what one accumulates, but by what one gives. Every individual, regardless of their means, has something to offer in service of others."

What began as a personal act of compassion has grown into a sustained community effort, feeding hundreds of people on any given occasion and reinforcing, meal after meal, the simple but powerful truth that feeding another human being is among the highest forms of service.

Narayana Seva — Annual Programme

Service to Humanity is Service to God

Under the Divine Guidance of Beloved Amma

"He who feeds the hungry feeds God. He who serves the suffering serves the Divine. This is not metaphor — it is the deepest truth of all spiritual paths."

Every year, the Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari Trust organises Narayana Seva — one of the most moving and large-scale acts of humanitarian service in the region. On this sacred day, more than 10,000 to 15,000 people who live on the margins of society are brought together under one great act of shared humanity: a freshly cooked, lovingly prepared meal, offered with open hands and an open heart.

This is not charity in the transactional sense. It is the offering of food — the most primary act of human care — to people who are too often rendered invisible by the world they live in. And it is led, personally and with her own hands, by Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari — beloved Amma — who on this day serves thousands of people, one by one, with the same tenderness she would offer her own children.

The ancient scripture, the Taittiriya Upanishad, declares: Annam Brahma — food is God. To offer food to another human being is therefore a sacred act of the highest order.

Communities Served

Who Narayana Seva Reaches

  • Residents of city slums — families living in overcrowded urban poverty
  • Inmates of the central jail — people often forgotten by society
  • Children in orphanages — young lives without the shelter of family
  • Students in schools for the deaf and mute — facing unique daily challenges
  • Families across multiple surrounding villages — rural communities with limited resources

Amma Serves with Her Own Hands

At the centre of all this vast activity is a single image that those who witness it never forget: Amma, standing at her home on this day, serving thousands of people with her own hands — hour after hour, with the same warmth and attentiveness for the thousandth person as for the first. This is perhaps the most powerful teaching of Narayana Seva: not the scale, but this image of a divine mother who refuses to allow her own elevation to create distance between herself and the most marginalised members of her community.

"When you bow down to serve another, you are not diminishing yourself. You are discovering the greatest height a human being can reach."

Rural Upliftment

Building the Foundations of a Self-Reliant Village

Guided by the teachings of Divine Mother Srimad Sai RajaRajeshwari, the Trust addresses the everyday needs that allow a community to grow with dignity — from classrooms to clean water.

Education — Opening Doors to a Brighter Future

Perhaps no gift is more transformative than education. Many village children, despite possessing talent, curiosity, and ambition, find their educational journeys cut short simply because their villages lack schools beyond a certain level, or because their families cannot afford the fees, books, uniforms, and incidental costs associated with higher schooling.

SSRRT has stepped in to bridge this gap through scholarships, financial assistance, mentorship, and direct sponsorship of tuition and study materials — enabling hundreds of children to pursue education that would otherwise have remained out of reach. For students aspiring to professional courses — engineering, medicine, nursing, teacher training — SSRRT's support can mean the difference between a life of limited opportunity and a career that transforms an entire family's circumstances for generations to come.

"A young woman who becomes the first in her family to graduate college often goes on to inspire her younger siblings and cousins, shifting the entire family's expectations around education."

Water Supply — Sustaining Life Through the Harshest Summers

Water is life, and nowhere is this truth felt more acutely than in the drought-prone regions of rural India during peak summer months. SSRRT has taken on this challenge with remarkable dedication, organizing and delivering drinking water supplies to numerous remote villages precisely during these critical periods of scarcity.

Through tanker deliveries, coordinated distribution efforts, and sustained logistical planning, the Trust ensures that families do not have to walk for miles under the scorching sun in search of water, nor resort to unsafe or contaminated sources that put their health at risk. Water deliveries are coordinated with village leaders to ensure equitable distribution, with priority given to the most vulnerable households first.

Bedding, Medicine & Crisis Relief

True compassion reveals itself most clearly in moments of crisis. During the COVID-19 pandemic and various natural disasters, the Trust mobilized swiftly to provide essential relief — bedding, blankets, medicines, and food supplies delivered directly to those who needed them most. What distinguishes SSRRT's crisis response is not merely the material aid provided, but the manner in which it is delivered — with urgency, without excessive bureaucracy, and with genuine care for the dignity of those receiving help.

Consecrated Space — Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari Ashram

Sacred Temples on the Banks of River Cauvery

Karekura, Mysore, Karnataka

[ Photograph — Aerial / entrance view of Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari Ashram, Karekura, Mysore ]

"This serene and beautiful place is blessed by Divine Mother Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari — an embodiment of compassion, devotion, and selfless service."

Nestled on the tranquil banks of the River Cauvery in Karekura, Mysore, Karnataka, the Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari Ashram is far more than a religious institution. It is a living spiritual ecosystem — a consecrated sanctuary where ancient divine energies converge, where the fragrance of sacred rituals mingles with the cool breeze of the river, and where every stone, every tree, and every temple pulsates with a devotional frequency that is palpable to all who enter.

The Ashram campus encompasses six consecrated temples, a sacred Goshala housing more than 300 cows, lush organic farmlands, and a community of devoted service that hums with purposeful activity throughout every hour of the day. Each temple was consecrated by Amma's own physical presence — her years of prayerful footsteps sanctifying the soil, her devoted worship charging the deities with living spiritual energy.

The River Cauvery — the Ganga of the South — flows along the Ashram's boundary, lending the entire campus an additional layer of sanctity. In ancient Hindu cosmology, river banks are considered among the most auspicious places for spiritual practice.

I. Krishna Maya Temple — The Temple of Lord Krishna

Govinda, Govinda — The One Who Gives Joy to All

Of all the divine incarnations in the vast tapestry of Hindu mythology, none has captured the human heart with quite the warmth, the beauty, and the multidimensional richness of Lord Krishna. He is the mischievous child of Vrindavan who stole butter and the hearts of all who saw him. He is the divine lover whose celestial flute calls every soul home. He is the warrior who stood beside Arjuna on the battlefield of Kurukshetra and delivered the immortal wisdom of the Bhagavad Gita.

The Krishna temple at the Ashram is a space of extraordinary gentleness and sweetness. Amma's relationship with Krishna is one of the deepest threads in her spiritual life. In the broader context of the Ashram's mission, the Krishna temple is profoundly appropriate — Krishna is the patron deity of cows, and the Ashram's Goshala is in many ways a physical embodiment of his teachings.

Worshipping at this temple carries spiritual benefits that operate on multiple levels. The Krishna temple offers the incomparable sweetness of Bhakti — devotional love. Many devotees report that simply sitting before the Krishna deity produces a natural expansion of the heart, a softening of defensive structures, a welling up of gratitude and tenderness.

[ Photograph — Lord Krishna Temple, Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari Ashram, Karekura ]

II. Shirdi Baba Temple — The Temple of Shirdi Sai Baba

Sabka Malik Ek — The Lord of All Is One
[ Photograph — Shirdi Sai Baba Temple, Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari Ashram, Karekura ]

Shirdi Sai Baba is one of the most universally beloved saints in Indian spiritual history — a figure whose transcendence of religious boundaries, whose radical compassion for every human being regardless of caste, creed, or religion, and whose miraculous works have made him an object of devotion for hundreds of millions worldwide.

The presence of a Shirdi Sai Baba temple at the Ashram carries a significance that is both personal and cosmic. Ancient Nadi readings describe Amma as "one equivalent to Shirdi Sai" in spiritual stature and in the quality of her mission. The same quality of unconditional compassion that radiates from accounts of Shirdi Sai Baba is experienced in Amma's presence.

Shirdi Sai Baba is known by the phrase that encapsulates his primary spiritual gift: Shraddha and Saburi — faith and patience. Devotees of Shirdi Sai consistently report that his grace operates in a thoroughly practical way — in the resolution of financial difficulties, healing of family conflicts, and provision of exactly what is needed at exactly the right moment.

III. Ganesha Sannidhi — The Temple of Lord Ganesha

Vighneshvara — The Remover of All Obstacles

Lord Ganesha — the elephant-headed son of Shiva and Parvati — is perhaps the most immediately recognisable deity in the entire Hindu pantheon. He is invoked at the beginning of every significant undertaking. In the Hindu understanding, nothing of significance begins without Ganesha's blessing, and with his blessing, no obstacle is insurmountable.

In the architecture of the Ashram's spiritual community, the Ganesha temple plays a foundational role. He is Prathamapujya — the first to be worshipped. His presence at the Ashram sets the spiritual tone for everything that follows — clearing the energetic field of obstacles and establishing auspiciousness.

Children, students, and young people often feel a particular affinity for Lord Ganesha, who is the patron deity of students and scholars. Many families from the surrounding villages make it a practice to bring their children to the Ganesha temple before the start of a new academic year.

[ Photograph — Lord Ganesha Temple, Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari Ashram, Karekura ]

IV. Subramanya Sannidhi — The Temple of Lord Subramanya

Murugan, Skanda, Karthikeya — The Eternal Warrior of the Spirit
[ Photograph — Lord Subramanya Temple, Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari Ashram, Karekura ]

Lord Subramanya — also known as Murugan, Skanda, Karthikeya — is among the most beloved and widely worshipped deities of South India. He is the second son of Shiva and Parvati, born to lead the army of the gods against darkness. He is depicted as a young man of surpassing beauty, holding a vel — a divine spear — that is simultaneously a weapon of spiritual warfare and a symbol of penetrating insight that cuts through the veils of illusion.

The presence of the Subramanya temple at the Ashram speaks to a dimension of spiritual life that is easy to overlook: the recognition that spiritual life is not passive. It requires the qualities of the warrior — courage, discipline, sustained effort, and the clarity to distinguish between what is real and what is mere appearance.

Devotees who worship regularly at Subramanya temples report a distinctive quality of blessing: a sharpening of the mind, a strengthening of willpower, a renewed sense of purpose and direction. The tradition also holds that Subramanya worship is among the most effective remedies for the malefic effects of the planet Kuja (Mars) in one's astrological chart.

V. Dattatreya Sannidhi — The Temple of Lord Dattatreya

Trimurti Incarnate — The Guru of Gurus

Lord Dattatreya is one of the most unique and philosophically profound of all Hindu deities — a figure who embodies the entire cosmic Trinity of Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva in a single form. He is revered above all as the Adi Guru — the first and original spiritual teacher. He is accompanied by four dogs (representing the four Vedas) and a cow (representing the earth and the nurturing principle).

The presence of a Dattatreya temple at an ashram is always a statement about the nature of the ashram's relationship to the guru principle — the understanding that spiritual growth requires the living transmission of grace from a realised teacher to a sincere student. Lord Dattatreya's presence consecrates and amplifies this guru principle.

Lord Dattatreya's grace is particularly associated with liberation from ancestral karmas — deeply entrenched patterns of behaviour and suffering passed down through family lines. Dattatreya worship, performed with sincerity, is one of the most powerful traditional means of addressing these ancestral patterns. Many devotees who come to this temple with these intentions report experiences of deep release — emotional, physical, and circumstantial.

[ Photograph — Lord Dattatreya Temple, Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari Ashram, Karekura ]

VI. Grand Shiva Temple — Lord of the Universe

Mahadeva on His Celestial Throne — A Vision of Cosmic Grandeur
[ Photograph — The Grand Shiva Temple and Nandi Mantapa, Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari Ashram, Karekura ]

Lord Shiva — Mahadeva, the Great God — is simultaneously the destroyer and the regenerator, the ascetic and the householder, the source of all arts and the lord of all yogas. He is known as Bholenath — "the innocent one" — the god who is most easily approached and most readily responds to sincere prayer.

The Shiva temple at the Ashram is described as one of the most distinctive features of the entire Ashram complex — a site that sets it apart from other sacred places in the region in both physical grandeur and spiritual intensity. Lord Shiva, along with his faithful Nandi, is installed upon a grand mantapa — an elaborately crafted throne of a scale and artistry that commands immediate awe from all who behold it.

Many devotees report that simply looking at this installation produces a spontaneous stillness of mind and an unexpected sense of having encountered something of tremendous significance. The statue and installation have been crafted with devotional precision and consecrated by Amma's hands.

The tradition holds that if you whisper your heartfelt prayer into Nandi's ear — with the specific, personal, earnest directness of one speaking to a trusted intermediary — that prayer is carried directly to Shiva's consciousness. Many devotees make this practice their first act upon entering the Shiva temple complex.

"Come. Sit. Be still. The Divine is here, and has been waiting for you."

Mother — Glimpses of Her Personality

The Divine Mother Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari

A wife, mother, guide, and a perfect renunciant. Her simple life and character reveal a unique ideal to mankind.

[ Photograph — Divine Mother Srimad Sai Rajarajeshwari (Amma) ]

The Human Aspect of Her Divine Personality

Mother having taken a human form has set a perfect example to an ordinary human being. She has had her education, got married, worked in a bank for 26 years, and financially secured herself with a pension after opting for voluntary retirement. In her daily life she never fails in her duty as a householder. She completes all her household work before attending to devotees or visiting the Ashram.

She is very soft and gentle in her talk to others. She is very warm and friendly to anyone who approaches her. The spirit of Motherhood is so powerfully operative in her that it endows her with readiness to serve others while silently foregoing her comforts and conveniences. Her interaction with devotees is filled with extreme tenderness, sweetness, and forbearance. It is this gentleness, dignity, and grace which never goes unnoticed by those who come to her.

She is an epitome of pristine purity, renunciation, and holy innocence. Amma as a human being has set an example to us that one need not be divine to be a good, perfect human being.

Divine Aspects of Her Personality

The life of every avatar is a celestial inscrutability. God plays the game of hide and seek through the veil of human personality. Mother was no exception to this. The myriad faces of the Mother explain her being. She can assume any form ranging from a gentle comforter to the fearless warrior. She can be tender and playful or terrifying and ferocious.

One of her primary attributes is to awaken us. The Divine Mother spiritually aims at transforming all men and women who come to her with the right attitude of mind into a spiritual giant. She is a reservoir of undaunted courage and exhibits this quality in critical situations of life.

"Mother demands total surrender on the part of her followers before she condescends to reveal herself in her divine state. Her fervent devotees must learn to see her presence in all things. She must become the bedrock and the meaning of their life. Then, and only then, can they aspire to experience her blessings in their totality."

To sum up her personality, it is a makeup of her Divinity, her Motherliness, her absorption in Swami, her simplicity, her childlike nature, her infinite endurance, limitless mercy, and absolute absence of egotism.

The Avataarhood

The Announcement of the Avataar

Ancient Nadi scriptures describe Amma in extraordinary terms. The Agastya Nadi places her in the same spiritual stature as the beloved saint Shirdi Sai Baba. The Budha Nadi records her as Omkara, Sreemkara, Hreemkara Roopini — embodying the three primordial cosmic sounds — and as Vasudeva Maya Durga, a form of the supreme divine feminine.

Amma was born on 29 March 1947 in Mangalore, Karnataka, India, into a deeply spiritual family that had prayed fervently for the Divine Mother to be born among them. Her earliest years were shaped by the presence of saints and sages, and by a household atmosphere of prayer, selflessness, and devotion. The revered saint Ramdas of Kanhangad, recognising her divine radiance, named her Lalitha — a sacred epithet of the Divine Mother, Lalitha Tripura Sundari.

For devotees, Amma is not merely a teacher of spiritual truths. She is a living embodiment of the divine — and her choices about how to live, what to build, and where to direct the Ashram's energies carry profound meaning.

The Naadi Readings

Ancient Palm-Leaf Prophecies

[ Photograph — Naadi palm-leaf manuscripts / readings ]

Ancient Nadi readings — palm-leaf manuscripts inscribed by sages thousands of years ago — describe Amma in extraordinary terms, foretelling her arrival and her mission with remarkable precision. These readings have been translated and preserved as part of the Ashram's spiritual heritage.

The Agastya Nadi places her in the same spiritual stature as Shirdi Sai Baba. The Budha Nadi records her as a form of the supreme divine feminine, embodying the three primordial cosmic sounds — Omkara, Sreemkara, Hreemkara Roopini — a Vasudeva Maya Durga.

For devotees who know both Amma and the Nadi tradition, these ancient prophecies are not surprising — they are felt in the quality of presence, the unconditional compassion, and the boundless energy of service that define her life.

Swami & Amma

The Shared Life and Teachings

Swami formed the centre of all the thoughts and activities of Amma. She was always a part and parcel of him, an eternal essence in his being and never having an existence separate from him. She had a tendency to hide her own towering greatness under the veil of Swami's glory.

It is touching to note that She herself being Adishakthi, always says that She is at the feet of Bhagwan Shri Sathya Sai Baba. Such is her love and humility. She made it clear that the purpose of Swami's and her life was not to attain anything for themselves but to sacrifice themselves for the enlightenment and salvation of others.

One can find the aspect of yoga and bhoga co-existing in her life. She has many times said, if one step of hers were in this world, the other would be a manifestation of her divinity.

Realized Beings

Saints and Sages of the Lineage

[ Photograph — Images of realized beings associated with the lineage ]

The Ashram honours the great saints and realized beings of the tradition associated with the lineage — including Shirdi Sai Baba, Bhagwan Shri Sathya Sai Baba, and the great masters of the Datta tradition. Each of these luminaries demonstrates that divinity is not an abstraction — it is a way of living.

Their teachings, stories, and samadhi continue to guide devotees who come to the Ashram seeking wisdom, healing, and the direct transmission of grace that flows from the guru-shishya parampara — the unbroken lineage of teacher to student that has sustained the Hindu dharmic tradition for millennia.

Profiles of realized beings associated with this tradition, including their teachings, samadhi, and continuing influence, are preserved in the Ashram's literature and shared with visiting devotees.
